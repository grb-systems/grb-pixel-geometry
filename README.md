# grb-pixel-geometry
A provisional G³GT framework for observational geometry: resolution, viewpoint, clearance from commitment, and perturbation introduced by observation.


Pixel Geometry
Observational Configuration, Clearance, and Perturbation

Status: Development Repository / Provisional / Pre-validation

Architecture: G³GT

Organization: Geometric Resource Bridging (GRB)

Scope: Public conceptual architecture, working definitions, falsifiers, developmental lineage, and experimental surface
Implementation: Implementation-neutral

Overview
Pixel Geometry is a G³GT framework for examining how observation is configured.
It asks:
How is an observation configured, what does that configuration make visible, how much room remains before observation becomes commitment, and what did the act of observing itself change?

The core object has four coordinates:
PIXEL GEOMETRY

SHIFT
resolution / scale

OFFSET
viewpoint

CLEARANCE
room before interpretation /
commitment / actuation

PERTURBATION
what observation itself changes

These are not truth levels.
They are not rankings of intelligence, depth, quality, or authority.
OBSERVATIONAL POSITION
≠
TRUTH RANK

VIEWPOINT
≠
AUTHORITY

The architecture treats observation as configured contact rather than a neutral view from nowhere.
1. Core Proposition
OBSERVATION
=
CONFIGURED CONTACT

not

VIEW FROM NOWHERE

Different observations may vary in:
resolution
viewpoint
clearance from commitment
measurement effect

Those relations should not be silently collapsed into one another.
SHIFT
≠
OFFSET
≠
CLEARANCE
≠
PERTURBATION

Pixel Geometry exists to make those differences inspectable.
2. Pixel Shift
Pixel Shift concerns observational resolution or scale.
It asks:
How closely are we looking?

A coarse Shift may reveal broad structure while hiding local variation.
A fine Shift may reveal local differentiation while increasing sensitivity to noise.
TOO COARSE
→ structure may disappear

TOO FINE
→ noise may acquire false importance

Pixel Shift is not equivalent to accuracy.
3. Pixel Offset
Pixel Offset concerns observational position or viewpoint.
It asks:
From where are we looking?

A relation may become available from one Offset and remain unavailable from another while the underlying locality remains substantially the same.
NEW TO VIEWPOINT
≠
NEW TO SYSTEM

Pixel Offset is not distance from truth.
It is observational displacement under preserved address.
4. Pixel Clearance
Pixel Clearance concerns the room maintained between observation and stronger commitment.
It asks:
How quickly must this observation become interpretation, decision, or action?

A compact form is:
I SEE SOMETHING
        ↓
    CLEARANCE
        ↓
I KNOW WHAT IT IS

Clearance preserves an interval in which observation can matter without yet requiring settled meaning.
CLEARANCE
≠
UNCERTAINTY

CLEARANCE
≠
TRUTH

CLEARANCE
≠
WARRANT

CLEARANCE
≠
AUTHORITY

Pixel Clearance is an observational condition.
Whether Clearance Geometry later deserves a broader architecture beyond Pixel remains unresolved.
PIXEL CLEARANCE
≠
GENERAL CLEARANCE GEOMETRY

That seam is intentionally left open.
5. Pixel Perturbation
Pixel Perturbation concerns change introduced by obtaining the observation.
It asks:
What did looking do to what was looked at?

Potential sources include:
measurement
monitoring
naming
summarization
routing
repeated attention
reporting
instrumentation

Therefore:
OBSERVATION
≠
ZERO-CARRY

Pixel Perturbation does not claim that every act of observation materially changes its target.
It keeps the possibility inspectable.

7. Detection–Actuation Collapse
One failure mode is:
OBSERVATION
→
IMMEDIATE INTERPRETATION
→
IMMEDIATE CONTROL

or:
DETECTION
→
ACTUATION COLLAPSE

Pixel Clearance helps keep those stages distinct.
DETECTION
≠
IDENTIFICATION

IDENTIFICATION
≠
AUTHORIZATION

AUTHORIZATION
≠
ACTUATION

Observation may justify further inspection without justifying immediate intervention.
8. Refraction and Return
Pixel Geometry assumes that changing observational configuration may change what becomes legible.
That does not make one configuration intrinsically superior.
A productive traversal may look like:
SOURCE LOCALITY
↓
CHANGE SHIFT / OFFSET / CLEARANCE
↓
OBSERVE DIFFERENT RELATION
↓
HARVEST CANDIDATE DISTINCTION
↓
RETURN
↓
REQUALIFY

Wide clearance does not create exemption from later qualification.
WIDE CLEARANCE
→ candidate formation

RETURN
→ structural sifting

CLAIM / ACTION
→ only when warranted

A useful Pixel move should preserve enough of the source address for comparison on return.
9. Plural Observation
Multiple observers do not automatically create multiple observational geometries.
Five observers with substantially identical context, role, instrumentation, and framing may reproduce the same Pixel five times.
MULTIPLE OBSERVERS
≠
OBSERVATIONAL PLURALITY

Plural observation requires meaningful differentiation in how observation is configured.
A wider view also does not automatically deserve greater authority.
WIDER VIEW
≠
HIGHER AUTHORITY

MORE INFORMATION
≠
BETTER LOCAL OBSERVATION

This is a Pixel-specific constraint against observational sovereignty.
10. Extensions
The following concepts directly elaborate Pixel relations but are not required to define the four-coordinate core.
Micro-Clearance
Micro-Clearance is concerned with the smallest warranted local change needed to preserve differentiation, movement, and return.
MICRO-CLEARANCE
=
minimum local adjustment
that preserves viable relation
without premature merger

Full treatment belongs outside the main README.
Clearance Reserve
Clearance Reserve distinguishes room currently available from room deliberately left unconsumed.
CLEARANCE
=
room presently available

RESERVE
=
some capacity deliberately
left uncommitted

This supports a negative-space question:
Where could another relation still form without displacing everything that already stands?

Clearance Reserve remains an extension, not a fifth Pixel coordinate.
Gain Provenance
developmental handle: Pixel 11
Gain Provenance asks not only whether a relation is strong, but how it became easier to see, hold, route, repeat, or carry.
IS R STRONG?
≠
WHY IS R GETTING STRONGER?

It asks:
WHAT IS GAINING?

WHY?

WHAT IS MAKING IT EASIER
TO CARRY FORWARD?

WHAT POSSIBILITY SPACE
IS THAT GAIN CONSUMING?

The developmental name Pixel 11 is retained for lineage.
It should not be read as a higher ordinal Pixel state.
11. Companion Geometry: N-Spoke
N-Spoke is related to Pixel Geometry but is not a Pixel coordinate.
PIXEL
asks:
how are we looking?

N-SPOKE
asks:
how many differentiated routes
remain available?

Pixel configures observation.
N-Spoke configures plurality or route resolution.
PIXEL
≠
N-SPOKE

ADJACENCY
≠
OWNERSHIP

The relationship may be experimentally useful, but no calibrated Pixel–N coupling law is claimed here.
12. When Pixel Geometry Is Not Needed
Not every change in wording, scale, framing, uncertainty, or perspective requires Pixel machinery.
Ordinary cases may remain ordinary.
Pixel Geometry becomes useful when observational configuration materially changes what can be seen, compared, qualified, or acted upon.
A framework that assigns special Pixel significance to every ordinary shift loses discriminating value.
IF PIXEL EXPLAINS EVERYTHING
PIXEL EXPLAINS NOTHING

13. Falsification Surface
Pixel Geometry should remain vulnerable to disconfirmation.
Coordinate Independence Test
Can Shift, Offset, Clearance, and Perturbation be varied independently enough to justify separate names?
If not, the decomposition may be unnecessarily complex.
Offset Test
Does changing viewpoint reveal relations unavailable from the original position while preserving enough source address for comparison?
If not, the claimed Offset may be rhetorical rather than functional.
Clearance Test
Does changing the distance between observation and commitment materially affect premature interpretation, narrowing, or actuation?
If not, Clearance may merely rename uncertainty.
Perturbation Test
Can the observation procedure itself be shown to alter the observed formation?
If not, a Perturbation account may be unnecessary for that case.
Plural Observation Test
Do nominally separate observers actually create differentiated observational geometries?
If not:
MORE OBSERVERS
≠
MORE OBSERVATIONAL INFORMATION

14. Minimal Experimental Surface
A simple Pixel experiment may begin with:
P₀
↓
SMALL REVERSIBLE PIXEL CHANGE
↓
P₁
↓
COMPARE

Possible changes include:
resolution
viewpoint
clearance
instrumentation
telemetry
temporal window
role-local access

Observe:
what became visible?

what disappeared?

what stayed invariant?

what was viewpoint-specific?

what changed because of measurement?

what remained recoverable on return?

The purpose is not to generate interesting observations.
It is to test whether the Pixel distinctions do real explanatory work.
15. Core Research Questions
1. Can Shift, Offset, Clearance, and Perturbation be experimentally separated?
2. When does Offset reveal relation rather than merely new description?
3. Can Clearance be distinguished from uncertainty?
4. Can Perturbation be distinguished from ordinary system variation?
5. Do multiple observers actually create different observational geometries?
Further extension and adjacency questions belong in RESEARCH.md.
16. Developmental Lineage
Pixel Geometry did not appear fully formed.
The current architecture appears to braid at least two developmental lines.
PERCEPTUAL DIFFERENCE
→ one-pixel shift
→ Pixel Shift / Offset

RELATIONAL ROOM
→ minimum clearance
→ clearance engineering
→ micro-clearance
→ reserve

LATER DIFFERENTIATION / BRAID
→ Pixel Geometry

Additional developments around Refraction, Gain Provenance / Pixel 11, Swarm, Agent Geometry, and N-Spoke later elaborated or tested parts of this family.
The mature architecture should not be back-projected into earlier work.
EARLY DEVELOPMENTAL ROOT
≠
LATER FORMULATION

ANTECEDENT RESEMBLANCE
≠
ANTECEDENT COMPLETION

Local developmental dates document internal chronology.
They are not equivalent to public publication dates.

18. Non-Claims
Pixel Geometry is not presented as:
a theory of truth
a hierarchy of cognitive depth
a hidden-model mechanism
a direct token-control system
a calibrated runtime controller
a mathematical law of cognition
a theory of consciousness
a divination system
a planning architecture
a proof of alignment

It does not claim:
- that higher Pixel values are better;
- that greater clearance produces greater truth;
- that symbolic exploration has special epistemic privilege;
- that every observation perturbs its target;
- that every distributed system requires differentiated Pixels;
- that Pixel 11 is a literal eleventh internal model state;
- that Pixel and N-Spoke form a calibrated control equation;
- that recurring salience establishes authority;
- that observation should remain open indefinitely.
19. Public Scope and Implementation Boundary
This repository specifies public conceptual architecture.
It does not claim or require disclosure of:
implementation-specific thresholds
adaptive policies
deployment heuristics
hidden model-state control mechanisms

No mature private runtime implementation is implied by this boundary.
The repository is intended to remain useful at the architectural level whether or not any later implementation adopts its vocabulary directly.

21. Operational Compression
VARY RESOLUTION.

VARY VIEWPOINT.

PRESERVE CLEARANCE.

TRACK PERTURBATION.

RETURN.

REQUALIFY.

Or:
Look from somewhere. Leave room before claim. Notice what looking changes. Return before visibility becomes warrant.

Guiding Observation
A small change in observational position can make a relation available without granting authority to name, close, or act on it.

And:
Pixel Geometry asks not only what was observed, but at what resolution, from what viewpoint, with how much clearance from commitment, and at what observational cost to the thing observed.
